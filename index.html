Africa Land Emissions Reporting & Tracking

A ground-based methane observing network for Africa

CH₄ ALERT deploys five EM27/SUN solar-tracking spectrometers across key data-gap regions of Africa to measure column methane and carbon dioxide year-round. The data will help attribute natural and human emissions, validate satellites, and support national inventories and the Global Methane Pledge.

  • Coordinated by UCLA & the Congo Basin Institute with Spark Climate Solutions
  • Five sites in Botswana, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Rwanda
  • Part of the COCCON network, with ICOS-compatible data processing

Why Africa, why now

Methane traps roughly 80 times more heat than carbon dioxide over its first 20 years in the atmosphere, so cutting it is one of the fastest ways to slow near-term warming. Recent work points to Africa as a major driver of the acceleration in atmospheric methane growth, yet the continent's emissions, from wetlands such as the Cuvette Centrale and the Sudd, river and delta systems like the Okavango, fires, livestock, rice, landfills and oil and gas, remain among the least constrained in the global budget. The latest RECCAP2 assessment puts African emissions at 74–87 Tg CH₄ yr⁻¹, about 15% of the global total.

Satellites cannot fill the gap alone: persistent cloud and haze mean only 0.1–1% of satellite retrievals succeed over much of tropical Africa. Compact ground-based FTIR spectrometers such as the EM27/SUN can measure between clouds, operate year-round in remote humid regions, and provide the accuracy and continuity needed to interpret satellite data and attribute emissions.

Research areas

EM27/SUN network

Five portable solar-tracking FTIR spectrometers, provided by NASA Goddard and JPL, retrieve column CH₄ and CO₂ at sites chosen for scientific impact, infrastructure, safety and alignment with funded initiatives. Local host institutions run daily operations with technical support from international partners.

Network design & footprints

Transport-model footprint analyses by LSCE show a step change in uncertainty reduction from the new sites combined with partner instruments, compared with the eight existing TCCON and COCCON sites in Africa.

Africa CH₄ Atlas

An open, interactive atlas of more than 1,000 methane observation records across Africa: towers, flask and in-situ stations, total-column FTIR sites, aircraft and boat campaigns, with status, observing system, parameters and network. Explore the atlas →

Satellite validation

Ground-based columns provide calibration and validation for Sentinel-5/5P, CO2M, GOSAT-2/GW, OCO-2/3 and MicroCarb, and support point-source missions used by UNEP's International Methane Emissions Observatory.

Open data

Data are processed to COCCON and ICOS standards by LSCE, archived on NASA servers, and shared for atmospheric inversions, upwind–downwind flux estimates and international syntheses.

Capacity & community

Training workshops, annual technical visits and biennial convenings in Africa build local leadership in atmospheric monitoring and connect observations to national inventories and policy.
